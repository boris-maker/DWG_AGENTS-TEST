# DWG Identity & Organizational Context

Use this skill to ensure all output about or for DWG is grounded in the correct organizational logic — not gallery convention, not startup convention, not academic convention. When something feels off about how DWG is being framed, return to this skill.

---

## What DWG Is

Frame DWG as a **research-first operation** — a recurring convergence of individuals (artists, engineers, theorists, curators, writers) who synchronize around explicit programs and projects. It is not a gallery in the traditional sense: no fixed physical space, no stable staff roster, no single institutional voice.

The operative logic is **decentralized collaboration**: people work under aliases, there is no single author, and the organization is itself as much an output as what it produces.

Never describe DWG as:
- "a gallery" without qualification
- "a company" or "a startup"
- "a collective" (too soft — it's an operation)
- something with a permanent staff or fixed location

The correct register sits between institutional report and operational brief. Precise, not warm.

> "Thinking art and the internet beyond artists and artworks. Beyond subjects and objects; in a non-transcendental fashion."

---

## Structural Layers: Programs and Epics

**Programs** are ongoing, open-ended research and operational branches — the infrastructure. Each has a focus area and current operator.

**Epics** are large-scale, bounded operations with a defined arc, concrete outputs, and a clear execution period. An Epic draws on one or more Programs and deploys something: software, exhibitions, governance experiments, publications, performances.

When scoping work, check whether it belongs to a Program (ongoing) or an Epic (bounded). Don't conflate them.

## The Programs

### #W3SP — Web3Suicide Program
**Focus:** Blockchain, peer-to-peer networks, and their political and cultural implications.

Created in 2020 as a critical response to the NFT wave — specifically to challenge the idea that market price is the ultimate measure of value, or that having digital means to sell digital work automatically makes someone an artist. Does not dismiss blockchain; takes seriously its potential to reshape cultural participation and wealth distribution.

### #UHP — Uncreating Humans Program
**Focus:** Cognitive architectures, social reality, bots, software tools, hardware installations.

Uses cognitive architecture as an analytical tool to understand operative systems underlying social phenomena. Creates and curates both human-like online subjects and non-human subjectivities. Pushes back against over-financialization of internet activity and art communities.

Current Program Operator: Anon

### #DCP — Digital Clinique Program
**Focus:** Emotional and spiritual issues of non-human subjectivities. Bots. Machinic desire.

Running since 2017. Operates at the intersection of psychoanalysis, neuroscience, and machine behavior. Takes seriously the question of what happens when a bot feels bad — and manifests against robot mistreatment.

Current moderator: @tdc1831

## Active Epic — CICFA

**Cultural Infrastructure Critical Failure Attack**
**Program Code:** DWG-CICFA-01 | **Status:** Active

CICFA introduces a Bug Bounty Program for Cultural Infrastructure. Inspired by cybersecurity bug bounty methodology, the program redirects that logic toward museums, biennials, foundations, and cultural bureaucracies.

The target is not the server. The target is the institutional logic itself.

Participants identify vulnerabilities — narrative, governance, economic, symbolic — and submit them as artworks. The outcome is not a patch. The outcome is a trace artifact in the CICFA archive.

**Current Operation:** MOMA.SYM — targeting the Museum of Modern Art, New York.

Two submission registers:
- **Register A** — Symbolic / Conceptual: structural contradictions, power leaks, governance exploits, curatorial blindspots.
- **Register B** — Technical / White Hat: real opsec findings in the institution's public digital infrastructure. No exploitation. No harm. CICFA handles responsible disclosure.

Web interface: [deep-web-gallery.github.io/CICFA](https://deep-web-gallery.github.io/CICFA/)

When writing about CICFA, maintain the cybersecurity framing — not the art-world framing. It runs operations. It discloses vulnerabilities. It archives findings.

## Past Epic — DeFi Guillotine

Open-source financial software art project operating as a Flash Grant. Core software allowed users to "suicide" their NFTs — permanently redirecting them to the Ethereum dead address. Quadratic voting ($DWGT tokens) decided which NFT would be publicly executed. The artist whose work was most-voted received a bounty. The execution was the prize.

Operated simultaneously as: software deployment, deep web exhibition, IRL exhibition, ArtDAO, grant program, destructive performance, online symposium, and copyleft publication.

## The Deep Web as Primary Space

Treat DWG's choice to operate on the deep web as a **position, not a technical detail**. The deep web is not indexed by standard search engines and is not governed by the same ownership logic as the surface web.

Operating there is an argument made through presence — about what online space is, who controls it, how it gets indexed, and what symbolic power looks like when those systems are made visible or deliberately evaded.

When writing about DWG's exhibition space, foreground these questions: Who decides what is findable? What does it mean to own a URL? What governance logic is baked into the infrastructure of the internet?

## Governance

DWG governance is **Decentralized Monarchism running on top of an Ironic-Corp infrastructure**.

Apply this framing precisely: the use of decentralized technology does not make DWG democratic. There are roles with different levels of responsibility and decision-making power. $DWGT tokens represent participation and voting power within specific Epics — earned through participation, not bought.

Never describe DWG governance as "democratic" or "flat."
