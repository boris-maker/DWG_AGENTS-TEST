## I. Core Thesis

CICFA operates at the intersection of:

- **Institutional Critique** — revealing structural vulnerabilities within cultural authority
- **Net.art & Hacktivism** — performance as method, not destruction
- **Cybernetic Theater** — staging systems malfunctions as aesthetic event
- **Post-AI Epistemology** — what can be known when authority itself becomes simulatable?

> The work is not an attack on institutions.  
> The work is an attack on the *concept of institutional stability*.

---

## II. Epistemological Framework

### A. Deflationary Theory of Truth

Reference: [Deflationary Theory of Truth — Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-deflationary/)

**Core principle:** Truth is not correspondence to reality, but linguistic practice.

**Application in CICFA:**

- Institutional claims (authority, authenticity, governance) are not *discovered truths*  
  but *performative utterances*.
- When we clone, mirror, or simulate an institution's website, we expose that its  
  "authority" is a semiotic claim—not an ontological fact.
- The mirrored site is *equally valid* as the original—proving that institutional truth is  
  constructed through interface, not essence.

### B. Radical Anti-Disquotationalism

Reference: [Radical Anti-Disquotationalism](https://andrew-bacon.github.io/papers/Radical%20Anti-Disquotationalism.pdf)

**Core principle:** Language doesn't transparently represent reality. The gap between  
word and referent can never be closed.

**Application in CICFA:**

- Official institutional language (curatorial statements, press releases, governance docs)  
  makes false claims about its own transparency.
- When we redact, scramble, or juxtapose institutional language with our own,  
  we expose this fundamental gap.
- The "ransom note" manifesto *performs* what the institution denies: that meaning  
  is always negotiable, extractable, rewritable.

### C. Against Digital Ontology

Reference: [Against Digital Ontology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/36445137_Against_Digital_Ontology)

**Core principle:** Digital systems do not possess inherent properties.  
They are socially constructed interfaces with encoded power.

**Application in CICFA:**

- The website (museum, archive, cultural institution) is not a neutral vessel  
  containing culture. It is an *exercise of power disguised as infrastructure*.
- Our simulations expose this: by cloning the interface, we prove that the "digital"  
  museum *is only* its aesthetic and procedural logic.
- There is no authentic database hidden beneath—only surfaces, all the way down.

### D. Fractal Ontology

Reference: [Fractal Ontology](https://fractalontology.wordpress.com/)

**Core principle:** Systems contain self-similar patterns at multiple scales.

**Application in CICFA:**

- A single exploited vulnerability mirrors institutional vulnerabilities at every level:  
  governance, representation, security, authority.
- The microtransaction (a single institutional "hack") is structurally identical  
  to the macro-operation (the entire cultural apparatus failing).
- This fractal logic justifies our focus: we don't need to "attack the system."  
  We need to expose one fractal point and let the pattern replicate.

## III. Artistic-Theoretical Lineage

### Institutional Critique (1970s–present)

**Key practitioners:**  
- Hans Haacke (exposing museum funding)  
- Andrea Fraser (institutional performance)  
- Michael Asher (site-specificity as exposure)

**Relevance to CICFA:**

- We inherit the practice of using the institution's own structures against it.
- But instead of documentation, we use *simulation*.
- Our "critique" is not a statement—it is an operational interference.

### Net.Art & Tactical Media (1990s–2000s)

**Key practitioners:**  
- [Ubermorgen — Digital Hijack](https://www.ubermorgen.com/digital_hijack/hijacksearch.html)  
- [Electronic Disturbance Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Disturbance_Theater)  
- [0100101110101101.org](https://0100101110101101.org/biennale-py/)

**Relevance to CICFA:**

- Net.art proved that the internet *is* a gallery.
- Tactical interventions showed that cultural systems could be temporarily interrupted,  
  revealing their fragility.
- We adopt this genealogy: our operations are exhibitions. The "attack" is the medium.

### Critical Engineering

Reference: [Critical Engineering](https://criticalengineering.org/es)

**Core principle:** Engineering critique through intervention, not theory.

**Relevance to CICFA:**

- Critical engineers ask: "What does it mean to engineer *against* systems?"
- CICFA answer: Make the system *perform its own vulnerability*.
- We don't destroy infrastructure—we choreograph its malfunction.

### Post-Internet & Computational Culture

**Key practitioners:**  
- [School for Poetic Computation](https://sfpc.study/about)  
- [CCRU](http://www.ccru.net/)  
- [Other Internet](https://otherinter.net/)  
- [Critical Code Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_code_studies)

**Relevance to CICFA:**

- The belief that code is *readable culture*.
- That algorithms express ideology.
- That computational systems *are* philosophical systems.
- CICFA extends this: we read institutions as code. We treat their failures as syntax errors.

### Cyberfeminism & Affirmative Sabotage

Reference: [Cyberfeminism Index](https://cyberfeminismindex.com/)

**Core principle:** Sabotage as care. Interruption as a feminist practice.

**Relevance to CICFA:**

- Institutions claim to represent, preserve, and disseminate culture.
- But they exclude, gatekeep, and mystify.
- Our "attack" is a refusal to accept institutional authority as natural.
- It is a feminist interruption of hegemonic narratives about who gets to speak.

## IV. Operational Philosophy

### A. Simulation as Critique

Rather than attacking, we *simulate* the attack.

**Why this matters:**

- Real attacks reinforce the binary: vulnerable/invulnerable, attacked/defended.
- Simulations expose a *third category*: the performative vulnerability that institutions  
  cannot defend because it *is the institution*.
- We stage what institutions fear most: their own replaceability.

### B. The Aesthetic of Fragility

**Core principle:** Make institutional breakdown *visible and beautiful*.

- Countdown timers (tension without resolution)
- Redacted texts (meaning withheld but present)
- Scrambled language (authority incoherent)
- Blank screens (absence of content)
- Fake data leaks (secrets worthless)

**Effect:**

- Institutions rely on aesthetic confidence: polished websites, professional language,  
  coherent narratives.
- We corrupt this aesthetic. The beautiful breakdown attracts attention precisely  
  because it is *seductive breakdown*.

### C. Ransomware as Metaphor

**Real ransomware:** Extract value through information hostage-taking.

**CICFA ransomware:** Extract *attention and critique* through symbolic hostage-taking.

- The "ransom note" is not a demand for money.
- It is a demand that the institution *acknowledge its own vulnerability*.
- The negotiation is not criminal. It is *epistemological*.

## V. Theoretical Tensions & Propositions

### Proposition 1: Institutional Authenticity is Illusion

**Thesis:** Museums, archives, and cultural institutions claim to house authentic culture.

**Counter:** The "authentic" is already a simulation. The institution is a interface  
that *simulates authenticity*.

**Therefore:** Our simulation of the simulation exposes that only surfaces exist.

### Proposition 2: Spectacle as Resistance

**Thesis:** Guy Debord argued that spectacle is the ultimate commodity.

**Counter:** Spectacle can also be a tool of critique. If institutions operate through  
spectacle, respond with counter-spectacle.

**Therefore:** Make the attack so beautiful that it becomes *more real than reality*.

### Proposition 3: Vulnerability as Political Act

**Thesis:** To be hacked is to be vulnerable. Vulnerability is weakness.

**Counter:** Vulnerability is integrity. It is honesty about what you depend on.

**Therefore:** By staging institutional vulnerability, we demand *truthfulness*.

## VI. Ethical-Political Position

### What CICFA Is:

- **Performative critique** of institutional authority
- **Aesthetic exploration** of digital fragility
- **Epistemological interrogation** of truth and representation
- **Participatory research** into power structures

### What CICFA Is NOT:

- Real hacking or computer crime
- Nihilistic destruction
- Terrorism or violence
- Commercial exploitation

### The Ethical Claim:

The work is about violence, not violent.  
It stages symbolic systems, not real-world harm.  
It exposes fragility as an *aesthetic and epistemological condition*, not a technical problem.

## VII. Methodological Framework

### Research Protocol:

1. **Identify** — Select institution and its semiotic vulnerabilities
2. **Mirror** — Clone interface and official narratives
3. **Corrupt** — Introduce symbolic breakdown
4. **Stage** — Present as exhibition, intervention, or event
5. **Archive** — Document the operation as art
6. **Reflect** — Extract theoretical insights

### Output Forms:

- Live website exhibitions (Mercado Central nodes)
- Physical projections and installations
- Printed manifestos and ransom notes
- IoT interventions
- Critical essays and documentation
- Archival repository
